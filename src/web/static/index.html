The Glaucoma ↔ Healthy ↔ Pathological Myopia Differential Boundary

In clinical ophthalmology, high axial myopia causes physical globe elongation that induces tilted optic discs and peripapillary atrophy (PPA). The exposed white sclera structurally mimics the neuroretinal rim thinning and pallor of advanced glaucoma. Simultaneously, healthy individuals with physiological large cupping are easily flagged as glaucomatous without 3D stereoscopic depth or OCT.

10-Class Case 1

Glaucoma Misclassified as Healthy

True: Glaucoma → Pred: Healthy (72.8%) Entropy: 0.952 [Flagged]

Ophthalmological Analysis: Shallow glaucomatous cupping without pronounced localized focal notching. Grad-CAM attention diffuses across the central macula rather than focusing on the vertical neuroretinal rim borders.

10-Class Case 2

Healthy Misclassified as Glaucoma

True: Healthy → Pred: Glaucoma (73.0%) Entropy: 0.655 [Flagged]

Ophthalmological Analysis: Patient presents with physiological macropapilla (naturally large cup-to-disc ratio > 0.65 with intact neural rim tissue). Without 3D depth, the convolutional filters trigger false optic nerve head activation.

10-Class Case 3

Pathological Myopia Misclassified as Glaucoma

True: Myopia → Pred: Glaucoma (87.6%) Entropy: 0.437 [Flagged]

Ophthalmological Analysis: Extensive temporal peripapillary crescent and oblique vessel insertion distort the anatomical disc borders. Grad-CAM confirms attention concentrated squarely on the scleral crescent.

4-Class Case 4

4-Class Glaucoma Misclassified as Normal

True: Glaucoma → Pred: Normal (95.6%) Entropy: 0.362 [Flagged]

Ophthalmological Analysis: Subtle localized RNFL wedge defect without marked pallor. The model's spatial attention focuses broadly along the superior vascular arcades rather than isolating the disc cup excavation.
